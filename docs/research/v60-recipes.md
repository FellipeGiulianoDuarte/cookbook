# Hario V60 canonical recipes — research report (compiled 2026-09-07)

Method note: every number below comes from a page fetched directly (URL given). YouTube pages do not render for fetch, so video titles/channels were confirmed via YouTube's oEmbed endpoint, and the numbers inside videos are taken from written transcriptions on the pages cited. Items that could not be verified are flagged explicitly.

---

## 1a. James Hoffmann — "The Ultimate V60 Technique"

- **Author:** James Hoffmann. **Primary source:** https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=AI4ynXzkSQo (title and channel confirmed via oEmbed; published 2020).
- **Written transcriptions used:** Hario USA (Hario-published) https://www.hario-usa.com/blogs/recipes-and-more-from-friends/james-hoffmann-uitimate-v60-technique ; Hario Europe ambassador Q&A (2021-10-12) https://www.hario-europe.com/blogs/hario-community/v60-ambassadors-james-hoffmann ; Honest Coffee Guide https://honestcoffeeguide.com/brew-recipes/james-hoffmann-v60/ ; Opie etc. scaled 1-cup transcription https://www.opie-etc.com/etc/commentary-on-james-hoffmans-ultimate-v60-technique-for-pour-over-coffee/
- **Dose / water / ratio:** 30 g / 500 g → 1:16.7 (60 g/L). (Several third-party pages round this to "1:15"; that is wrong.)
- **Grind:** "medium-fine" / "fine-medium" (no µm given).
- **Temperature:** Hario USA transcription: "the hotter, the better (especially with lighter roasts)". Hario Europe lists 97 °C; Honest Coffee Guide lists 100 °C; Gota/Unpacking list 95 °C. **Disagreement flag:** the video's own instruction is boiling/near-boiling for light roasts and lower for darker roasts.
- **Filter:** Hario V60 paper, rinsed with water just off the boil; plastic V60 02 recommended.
- **Steps (cumulative water):**
  - Prep: rinse filter, discard rinse water, add coffee, flatten bed, make a small well in the centre.
  - 0:00–0:10 Bloom: 60 g (2× dose), then swirl the brewer until grounds are evenly wet. Rest until 0:45.
  - 0:45–1:15 Pour to **300 g** (add 240 g; 60% of total brew water).
  - 1:15–1:45 Pour to **500 g** (add 200 g), slightly slower, keeping the cone topped up.
  - ~1:45 Stir once clockwise, once counter-clockwise with a spoon (knocks grounds off the paper).
  - When the water has dropped a little: gentle swirl to flatten the bed.
  - Drawdown complete by **~3:30**.
- **Scaling:** linear. Opie's scaled version for 15 g / 250 g is 30 g bloom, 150 g by 1:15, 250 g by 1:45. Hoffmann himself later said this scaled-down version underperforms at one cup, which motivated the 2022 recipe below.
- **When to use:** 2-cup batches (500 g) or larger; any roast, hotter water for lighter.

## 1b. James Hoffmann — "A Better 1 Cup V60 Technique" (2022)

- **Primary source:** https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=1oB1oDrDkHM (2022). Transcriptions: Opie etc. https://www.opie-etc.com/etc/commentary-on-james-hoffmanns-a-better-1-cup-v60-technique-for-pour-over-coffee/ ; Peter Thelander https://www.peterthelander.com/2023/08/27/a-better-1-cup-v60-technique/
- **Dose / water / ratio:** 15 g / 250 g → 1:16.7. Soft filtered water, freshly boiled.
- **Grind:** medium-fine, "as fine as you can go without bitterness/astringency" — finer than the Ultimate technique.
- **Temperature:** boiling for light roasts; cooler for darker. Opie transcribes 100 °C light / 96 °C medium / 90 °C dark — **flag: Opie's transcription, not verified against the video.**
- **Steps (cumulative):** five equal 50 g pours, each ~10 s:
  - 0:00–0:10 pour **50 g**, swirl, bloom until 0:45
  - 0:45–0:55 pour to **100 g**
  - 1:10–1:20 pour to **150 g**
  - 1:30–1:40 pour to **200 g**
  - 1:50–2:00 pour to **250 g**
  - gentle swirl; drawdown done ~**3:00**
- **Difference from Ultimate:** 5 pours (vs 2 main pours), bloom 50 g (~3.3× dose vs 2×), finer grind, no spoon stir.
- **Scaling:** designed for 15 g/250 g; not intended for large batches.

## 2. Tetsu Kasuya — 4:6 Method (2016 World Brewers Cup champion)

- **Primary sources:** Philocoffea (Kasuya's company), 2022-03-07: https://en.philocoffea.com/blogs/blog/coffee-brewing-method ; Philocoffea beginner guide 2021-09-27: https://en.philocoffea.com/blogs/blog/hario-v60-beginner-brewing-guide ; Hario UK ambassador post 2022-05-10: https://www.hario.co.uk/blogs/hario-ambassadors/hario-v60-recipe-interview-with-hario-ambassador-tetsu-kasuya ; Hario Europe Q&A 2023-02-06: https://www.hario-europe.com/blogs/hario-community/v60-ambassadors-tetsu-kasuya ; competition-recipe translation: https://japanesecoffeegear.com/tetsu-kasuya-world-brewers-cup-recipes-translated
- **Dose / water / ratio:** 20 g / 300 g → 1:15 (nominal; Philocoffea gives scaling rule: each pour = 3× dose, total water = 15× dose).
- **Grind:** coarse ("medium-coarse to coarse", kosher-salt texture) — deliberately coarser than other V60 recipes.
- **Temperature (Philocoffea):** light ~93 °C, medium ~88 °C, dark ~83 °C. Hario UK/Europe posts give 92 °C for Kasuya's own brew. Japanese Coffee Gear gives 92–94 / 88–91 / 85–88. **Flag: sources disagree by 2–3 °C per roast level; Philocoffea is the official one.**
- **Filter/brewer:** Hario V60 (Kasuya model) with Hario paper.
- **Structure:** total water split 40% (120 g) / 60% (180 g). First 40% in two pours controls taste; last 60% in 1–3 pours controls strength. Each pour goes in only after the previous one has almost fully drained ("~45 s intervals").
- **Standard schedule (Philocoffea):**
  - 0:00 **60 g** → 60 g
  - 0:45 60 g → **120 g**
  - 1:30 60 g → **180 g**
  - 2:15 60 g → **240 g**
  - 2:45 60 g → **300 g**
  - 3:30 remove dripper
- **Competition schedule (2016 Dublin, per Hario UK/Europe and JCG):** 0:00 50 g → 50; 0:45 70 g → 120; 1:30 60 g → 180; 2:10 (JCG: 2:15) 60 g → 240; 2:40 (JCG: 3:00) 60 g → 300; remove 3:30. **Flag:** Philocoffea puts pour 5 at 2:45; Hario UK/Europe at 2:40; JCG at 3:00.
- **Adjustment rules:**
  - First 40%: less water in pour 1 → sweeter (e.g. 50 + 70); more water in pour 1 → brighter/more acidic (e.g. 70 + 50); 60 + 60 balanced.
  - Last 60%: 1 pour (180) → lightest; 2 pours (90 + 90) → medium; 3 pours (60 × 3) → strongest. Philocoffea: for dark roasts start with 2 pours in the 60%.
- **Total time:** 3:30.

## 3. Scott Rao — V60 (bloom + pour + "Rao spin")

- **Current published recipe (Hario UK ambassador post, 2022-05-10):** https://www.hario.co.uk/blogs/hario-ambassadors/hario-v60-recipe-interview-with-hario-ambassador-scott-rao
  - Plastic V60 02; **20 g / 330 g at 97 °C**; medium-fine; standard Hario filters.
  - Make a "bird's nest" indent; bloom **60 g**, spin the bloom aggressively.
  - 0:40 pour to **200 g**, very gentle spin (<1 s) to close the rib channels.
  - When slurry is ~70% drained, pour to **330 g**, another gentle spin.
  - Pour from low height, near-vertical stream, across the whole slurry.
  - Total **4:00–4:30**; target extraction **22–24.5%**.
- **Original 2017 method (blog "V60 Video", 2017-09-15):** https://www.scottrao.com/blog/2017/9/14/v60-video — Rao has retitled that video "(old) V60 Method", so the 2017 single-pour version is superseded; the 2022 Hario UK recipe above is the current one.
- **Roast/temperature stance (blog 2024-02-26):** https://www.scottrao.com/blog/2024/2/26/how-to-approach-brewing-different-coffees — keep temperature, dose, ratio fixed; **change only grind** between coffees of similar roast level; filter TDS target 1.3–1.4%; down-dose only for high-fines coffees (Ethiopians, decafs). Rao advises not going below 20–22 g on a V60.

## 4. Matt Winton — Five-Pour V60 (2021 World Brewers Cup champion)

- **Primary source:** Hario Official Channel video "[HARIO] V60 Five-Pour Recipe - Matt Winton" https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=YIC-2nFQ7vM. Transcriptions: Honest Coffee Guide https://honestcoffeeguide.com/brew-recipes/matt-winton-v60-five-pour/ ; BeanBook https://beanbook.app/recipes/matt-winton--matt-winton-s-v60-5-pour-method__f8656c79-0f9d-46e5-98fe-8b4e14b69b80
- **Dose / water / ratio:** 20 g / 300 g → 1:15.
- **Grind:** coarse (Honest Coffee Guide says "medium"; Winton's own instruction is coarser than typical, adjust so total ≈3:30).
- **Temperature:** Honest Coffee Guide reports **two kettles: 93 °C for the bloom, 88 °C for the later pours**. BeanBook reports a single 93 °C. **Flag: the two-kettle version is his competition detail, single kettle is the home version.**
- **Brewer/filter:** Hario metal V60 02, Hario paper (rinsed).
- **Steps (five equal 60 g pours, each poured aggressively centre-then-outwards-then-back-to-centre, from height):**
  - 0:00 **60 g** bloom; wait ~30 s (some transcriptions 45 s)
  - ~0:30–0:45 → **120 g**, poured as soon as previous water has just finished dripping through
  - → **180 g** when drained
  - → **240 g** when drained
  - → **300 g** when drained
  - Drawdown complete ≈ **3:30**.
- **Notes:** no stirring/swirling — agitation comes from pour height. Honest Coffee Guide lists "total 6:50", which conflicts with every other source (likely a data error). Scales linearly (pour = 3× dose).

## 5. Lance Hedrick — V60 recipes

**Flag: there is no single canonical Lance Hedrick V60 recipe; he has published at least three.**

- **"The Last V60 Recipe You'll Ever Need"** (https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=PNFVCmxBjQQ; transcription http://beanbook.app/recipes/lance-hedrick--lance-hedrick-s-one-and-done-v60-recipe__15c20164-8b16-470a-9045-7452c1a4d129): 15 g / 225 g (1:15), 93 °C, medium-fine. 0:00 bloom **45 g** (3× dose); 0:30 second bloom +45 g → **90 g**; 1:00 pour to **225 g** at ~10 mL/s in small circles; done **2:30**. Gentle swirl at end optional. Emphasises hitting the time, not the grind number.
- **"The World's Best Pourover (with my Recipes)"** (https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=wZst-D3eUm0): 15 g / 250 g (1:16.7), 93 °C. 0:00 bloom 45 g; 0:45 optional second bloom 20–35 g if bed is dry/crusty; 1:30 main pour to **250 g**; done 2:30–3:00. Stated flow ~7 mL/s.
- **"Ultimate Pourover Recipe (any method)"** transcription https://www.coffeecalculator.app/blog/lance-hedrick-pour-over-recipe: 20 g; ratio by roast light 1:15–1:17, medium 1:15, dark 1:14; temperature light 100 °C / medium 92 °C / dark 85 °C; medium-fine; bloom 60 g 30 s with swirl; then to 100 g (0:30–1:00), 200 g (1:00–1:30), 300 g (1:30–2:00); done ~3:00.

## 6. Kurasu / Hario official / Onyx Coffee Lab

**Kurasu (Kyoto)**
- 2020 recipe https://kurasu.kyoto/blogs/kurasu-journal/how-to-brew-with-hario-v60-recipe-by-kurasu-kyoto (2020-05-28): 13 g / 200 g (1:15.4), **90–91 °C**, ceramic V60. 0:00 bloom **30 g**, 30 s; 0:30–1:00 pour to **100 g**; after 1:00 pour to **200 g**.

**Hario official** (https://global.hario.com/v60/v60series.html): rinse filter with hot water; bloom with **2× coffee weight**, wait **30 s**; then pour in 3 parts of **4× coffee weight** each (i.e. 14× total, e.g. 12 g → 168 g), spiral from centre, never onto the paper; total **within 3 minutes regardless of cup count**. Instruction manual says 10–12 g per 120 mL serving. No temperature specified.

**Onyx Coffee Lab**
- Generic V60 brew guide video https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=V30qg_pQ9mY (transcription https://beanbook.app/recipes/onyx-coffee-lab--onyx-coffee-lab-v60-brewguide__c0276b02-71b0-4485-8e64-a16dfc44ac2a): 15 g / 250 g (1:16.7), 210 °F (~99 °C), medium-fine. 0:00–0:30 bloom **50 g**, break clumps with a stick; 0:30–1:30 heavy tight centre circles to **150 g**; 1:30–2:30 to **250 g**; done **2:30**.
- Per-coffee Hario-cone recipes with µm (https://onyxcoffeelab.com/pages/brew-guides): e.g. "Geometry" 25 g / 400 g @ 205 °F, **725 µm**: 0:00 50 g; 0:30 160 g; 0:45 220 g; 1:05 280 g; 1:30 340 g; 2:00 400 g; drain 3:35. Their 20 g / 300 g @ 204 °F recipes use **550 µm**: 0:00 40 g; 0:30 120 g; 0:50 180 g; 1:10 240 g; 1:30 300 g; drain 2:30. Grind across their catalogue: 530–817 µm; temps 200–206 °F.

## 7. Adjusting for roast level and processing

| Source | Light | Medium | Dark | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Hoffmann (Ultimate/1-cup) | boiling, "hotter is better" | cooler | cooler still (Opie transcribes 90 °C, unverified) | grind finer for 1-cup |
| Kasuya / Philocoffea | ~93 °C | ~88 °C | ~83 °C; use 2 pours in the 60% | https://en.philocoffea.com/blogs/blog/coffee-brewing-method |
| Cafec (filter maker) | 92 °C (T-92) | 90 °C (T-90) | 83 °C (T-83) | https://cafec-jp.com/products/filterpaper/ |
| Lance Hedrick (Ultimate) | 100 °C, 1:15–1:17 | 92 °C, 1:15 | 85 °C, 1:14 | coffeecalculator.app transcription |
| Scott Rao (2024) | fixed temp (just-boiled kettle) | same | same | change only grind |
| Jonathan Gagné (Coffee ad Astra) | boiling; plastic V60; keep water above bed | — | "lower temperatures extract different flavour profiles" | https://coffeeadastra.com/2018/11/30/brewing-better-coffee/ |

**Processing:**
- Washed vs natural (The Roasters Pack, 2022-12-05, https://us.theroasterspack.com/blogs/news/how-to-best-brew-natural-coffees): naturals → grind slightly coarser, drop temperature (e.g. 96 → 92 °C), ratio 1:16.5, pour more gently; experimental ferments treated like naturals.
- Anaerobic (The Blind Coffee Roaster, https://www.theblindcoffeeroaster.com.au/blogs/news/how-to-brew-anaerobic-process-coffee): 88–91 °C, 750–800 µm (vs ~600 standard), 1:16.5–1:17, gentle centre pours, rest beans 3–4 weeks.
- Rao 2024: does **not** differentiate by process. **Flag: processing advice comes from roasters, not from Hoffmann/Rao/BH.**

## 9. Water temperature

- **SCA Golden Cup:** 93 °C ± 3 °C (200 °F ± 5 °F) at contact, i.e. 90–96 °C.
- **Hoffmann:** boiling/near-boiling for filter coffee, especially light roasts.
- **Dark roast 83–90 °C:** Kasuya 83 °C; Cafec 83 °C; Lance 85 °C; Opie's Hoffmann 1-cup 90 °C. Rao dissents (fixed temperature, change grind).

## 10. Filters

- **Hario tabbed vs untabbed** (Drip Roast https://www.driproast.com/best-v60-filters/): original **untabbed, Japan** — thin, rough both sides, fastest drainage, largely discontinued. **Tabbed Japan** — smoother interior, drawdown **+30–60 s** vs original. **Netherlands-made tabbed** — thickest, slowest, "prone to unpredictable clogging", papery taste.
- **Cafec** (https://cafec-jp.com/products/filterpaper/): Abaca / Abaca+: fast, all roasts. T-92 (light roast, 92 °C): slowest drawdown. T-90 (medium-dark, 90 °C): fastest. T-83 (dark, 83 °C): fast start then slows. Cafec rebranded 2024 to TH-1 = T-92, TH-2 = T-83, TH-3 = T-90.
- **Effect on recipes:** a slower paper needs a coarser grind to keep the time target; a fast paper allows grinding finer.

## 11. Golden Cup

- **SCA:** TDS **1.15–1.35%**, extraction **18–22%**, brew ratio 55 g/L ± 10%, 93 ± 3 °C.
- **SCAE (Europe):** TDS **1.20–1.45%**, extraction 18–22%.
